# Code of Conduct

Trust & Life Protocol discussions concern trust, safety, work, commerce, and public accountability. Disagreement is expected; personal hostility is not.

Participants are expected to:

- criticize ideas, evidence, and implementations rather than people;
- distinguish allegations from verified findings;
- avoid harassment, intimidation, doxxing, discriminatory abuse, or coordinated public shaming;
- respect privacy, confidentiality, and legitimate safety constraints;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest in trust-sensitive discussions;
- make a good-faith effort to correct errors when evidence changes.

Project spaces must not be used to publish personal data, secretly obtained private material, or unsupported accusations against identifiable workers or small businesses.

Reports about potential misconduct or unsafe practices should contain enough evidence for review while minimizing unnecessary personal information.

Maintainers may edit, hide, lock, or remove contributions that violate these principles and may restrict participation when necessary to protect contributors or the integrity of the review process.

Substantive moderation decisions should, where practical, be documented so that governance itself remains accountable.
