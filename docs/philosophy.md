# Project Philosophy

## 1. Trust should have reasons

Trust & Life begins from a simple premise: social and commercial trust is often necessary, but trust is stronger when people can inspect the reasons that justify it.

The protocol therefore does not aim to eliminate trust. It aims to reduce situations in which trust must be **blind**.

**Claim → Evidence → Verification → Trust**

A producer may claim that a product was kept within a required temperature range. A useful system does not merely repeat that claim. It records the relevant events, links the claim to temperature evidence, identifies the source of the evidence, shows known gaps, and allows independent review where appropriate.

## 2. Transparency is not total exposure

Transparency has moral and practical limits.

A trustworthy supply chain may need to expose production conditions without exposing workers' private conversations, faces, homes, rest areas, health information, or unrelated business information.

The project therefore distinguishes:

- **process transparency** — visibility into activities relevant to a claim;
- **personal surveillance** — persistent observation of identifiable people beyond what is necessary;
- **commercial disclosure** — publication of business information that may not be necessary for verification.

Trust & Life favors the first and minimizes the latter two.

## 3. Evidence has degrees

Evidence is not binary. A live camera may show a process but miss an important area. A sensor may be accurate but poorly calibrated. A signed report may prove who issued a document but not that every underlying observation was correct.

The system should therefore expose evidence strength, coverage, provenance, verification status, and limitations instead of collapsing everything into a single badge.

## 4. Trust is portable

A producer should not have to become dependent on one marketplace merely to make trustworthy conduct visible.

A conforming trust record should be usable across participating stores, websites, public registries, procurement systems, and consumer tools.

The protocol should make trust more portable than platform reputation.

## 5. Responsible conduct should become economically visible

Markets often reward what consumers can see. If responsible production is expensive but invisible, responsible producers may be forced to compete against lower standards on price alone.

Trust & Life attempts to make relevant conduct visible without requiring destructive price competition or exaggerated moral marketing.

The goal is not to guarantee higher prices. It is to allow evidence-backed quality and responsibility to become legitimate dimensions of competition.

## 6. Consumers are participants, not targets

A trustworthy commerce system should help people understand products rather than manipulate attention through artificial urgency, misleading scarcity, or anxiety-driven interfaces.

The project supports informed and proportionate consumption. Public education, traceability, and evidence should increase agency rather than pressure people to buy more.

## 7. Openness applies to the protocol itself

A project about transparency should itself be inspectable.

The normative rules, changes, limitations, conflicts, and governance decisions of Trust & Life should therefore be public and versioned wherever practical.
