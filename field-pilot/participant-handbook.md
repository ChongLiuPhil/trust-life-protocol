# Field Pilot Participant Handbook

This document explains what a real participant should expect before joining a Trust & Life pilot.

## What participation means

Participation means agreeing to a clearly bounded evidence/transparency workflow for a stated organization, facility, product/batch/process, time period, and profile version.

It does **not** mean:

- Trust & Life owns or operates the participant;
- every activity is continuously observed;
- every product is guaranteed safe;
- the participant receives permanent certification;
- unrelated facilities/products inherit the result;
- the participant must publish all internal data.

## What the participant should receive before joining

The operator should explain:

1. exact scope and profile version;
2. required and optional evidence categories;
3. which information is public, restricted, verifier-only, or not collected;
4. retention and access rules;
5. expected workload and technical integration;
6. independent verifier/laboratory role where applicable;
7. how gaps/outages are displayed;
8. how incidents can suspend public status;
9. correction and appeal procedures;
10. fees, if any, separately from the conformance decision.

## Data minimization

A participant should not be asked to publish trade secrets, worker personal data, private contacts, security-sensitive facility details, or unrelated documents merely to appear “transparent.” Equivalent lower-intrusion evidence should be considered when it can support the same requirement reliably.

## Monitoring

If process observation is used, the participant and affected workers should understand its purpose, zones, schedule/coverage, blind spots, retention, who can view it, and what is publicly exposed. Trust & Life does not require cameras in private areas and does not define worker surveillance as a trust goal.

## Findings

Each requirement should be explainable individually. A `gap`, `blocked`, or `manual-review` finding should identify what condition is missing or unresolved. A participant should not be required to purchase unrelated platform services to change the result.

## Corrections

If public information is factually wrong or out of date, the participant should have a documented correction route. Material changes should result in a superseding record rather than silent rewriting of historical decisions.

## Appeals

A participant may challenge an adverse or procedurally flawed decision through the declared appeal route. The appeal should be reviewed by a role with appropriate independence from the original disputed decision where feasible.

## Withdrawal

Participation may end or be withdrawn according to the pilot terms and applicable obligations. Withdrawal should stop current participation claims but does not necessarily erase lawful historical records that are needed to prevent misleading interpretation.

## Commercial neutrality

Marketplace ranking, advertising, sponsorship, consulting purchases, or commissions must not secretly modify machine-checkable conformance findings. Any commercial services should be disclosed separately from the protocol result.

## Before signing a real pilot agreement

The participant should review the actual legal agreement, local regulatory obligations, privacy/worker implications, evidence burden, security responsibilities, costs, and exit conditions with appropriate advisors. This open-source handbook is not legal advice or a substitute for required food-safety controls.
